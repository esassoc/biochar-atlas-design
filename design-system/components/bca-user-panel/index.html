Record panel

A record read as a slide-in panel over the list it came from, rather than a page you navigate away to. Wraps the hub's esa-side-dialog and adds the three things it leaves to its host: which record is showing, the URL contract, and the z-index.

Spoke .astro import BcaUserPanel from '../../components/bca/bca-user-panel.astro';

Preview

Opening a record does not leave the list. The panel overlays the grid at 520px with a scrim behind it; the grid keeps its scroll position, its sort and its filters, which is the whole reason for the pattern.

The URL contract

A record that loses its own route must not lose its link. Open state lives in ?user=<id> — pushed on open, stripped on close, honoured on load and on popstate. Escape then Back reopens the record you just closed, and a pasted URL lands with the panel already open.

Two parts are easy to leave out and both are load-bearing. Opens driven by history pass push: false, or every Back press adds an entry. And popstate → close() fires the lego's close event, whose handler would push a second entry for a navigation the user already made — so a re-entrancy flag guards it.

Triggers stay real anchors carrying href="?user=<id>" with the left click intercepted. That is what makes the link genuinely shareable rather than a click handler wearing an underline.

Anatomy

Every record is server-rendered into the panel and hidden; opening one unhides it. Nothing is built at runtime, so the same bca-record-header, bca-field-group and bca-field the record page used render unchanged.

The identity band goes in the header slot, the field groups in the default slot at their flush variant — three outlined cards inside a bordered 520px drawer is four nested rectangles for one record — and the record's actions in the footer slot. That slot has to sit on a real element, not an Astro <Fragment>: the Fragment unwraps before the DOM, the attribute is lost, and the lego's .footer:not(:has(*)) rule then collapses an empty bar while the button falls into the body.

Stacking: the write surface

Editing opens a second panel over the record rather than a modal beside it. Two overlay grammars on one screen for one record is the thing being avoided; the stack keeps one grammar and shows the relationship — the record you were reading is still there, one card back.

Three things separate the layers, and the geometry is the weakest of them. The under card re-points --side-dialog-inset to 40px, which the lego animates, so it slides left and peeks out by 24px — the peek is the difference between the two insets, and 24px is a hard ceiling because it is the lego's own body padding. One pixel more and the strip shows the first sliver of every label instead of the card's own edge.

A 24px strip is not enough on its own, so the top card also brings a scrim and a leading edge. --side-dialog-backdrop-bg goes to 30% black — the card underneath dims, the way the page dims when the record opens, but at well under the page scrim's 50% because this is one step of depth rather than the app going away. That is the primary lever. Sampled off the render, the profile across the seam runs #484847 for the page, #aeaeab for the card underneath and #fafaf7 for the top card — two hard steps, which is a bigger value break than any shadow can draw.

The shadow draws the line the scrim's value break implies. esa-side-dialog hardcodes box-shadow to --shadow-400 and ships no shadow hook of its own, and the shipped --shadow-400 is 0 8px 32px -8px rgba(0,0,0,.08) — straight down, at 8%. On a panel pinned to the right edge, the only edge you see is the left one, and a downward shadow draws nothing there. Both panels re-point --shadow-400 to --bca-panel-shadow, which throws left and keeps its density near the edge so the peek beside it stays legible.

It holds at three levels. Each panel's backdrop dims everything below it, so the scrims compose without any depth bookkeeping — a third card puts the record at 51%, the editor at 30% and itself at zero, which stays monotonic and readable. The insets are the only part that would need a third value.

The backdrop has to be the component token and not --color-backdrop. The lego reads var(--side-dialog-backdrop-bg, var(--color-backdrop, …)), and component-tokens.css already sets the component token at :root from the generic one — so the substitution has happened by the time the value reaches a panel, and the generic token has nothing left to redirect.

Escape unwinds one level at a time, because the lego binds Escape to its own panel's keydown rather than to the document. Handing focus back is the part that does need code: the lego stores document.activeElement at open and calls .focus() on it at close, and when the record panel is open that reports the <esa-side-dialog> host — real focus is inside its shadow root. Focusing a host with no tabindex is a no-op, so focus fell to <body> and the second Escape did nothing. The editor's close handler restores focus to the control that opened it instead.

The URL carries the record and not the depth. Beacon's drawers are not addressable at all; a link that reopened someone else's half-filled edit form would be a worse contract than no link.

Widths match at 520px. A wider top card would cover the peek entirely and the two would read as one panel that changed its mind.

Density

The panel runs at beacon-design's drawer scale — a --type-size-400 name, 15px values, 14px labels — set once as a block of --record-* custom properties on the two slot scopes. Every record component inside reads them, so a component nested three deep picks up the scale without a density prop in three signatures.

The name takes Inter semibold rather than a smaller Crimson Pro. The display serif is the page title's voice, one level up on the same screen, and a second serif line inside a drawer over that page competes with it instead of sitting under it.

Behaviour the lego already handles

Focus moves into the panel on open and is restored on close, Tab wraps inside it, and ✕ / Escape / backdrop all resolve to one close event. It carries role="dialog" and aria-modal="true". It does not apply inert to the page behind — the trap is JS, not native — so background content stays in the accessibility tree.

API

esa-side-dialog

PropTypeDefaultDescription
open boolean false Reflected. Prefer show() / close() — they run the animation and the focus handling.
heading string '' Renders as the panel title, and is the aria-label. Set it even when you slot a header, or the panel loses its accessible name.
position 'left' | 'right' 'right' Which edge it enters from.
size 'sm' | 'md' | 'lg' 'md' 320 / 400 / 520px. Override --_width directly for anything else.
show-close-button boolean true The ✕ in the header.
close CustomEvent — Fires on ✕, Escape and backdrop click alike — one handler covers all three. There is no matching open event.

CSS hooks

PropTypeDefaultDescription
--_width length per size The one to set. 520px here, and the edit panel that stacks over it matches — a stack needs equal widths, because the under card peeks by sliding rather than by being wider.
--z-modal / --z-modal-backdrop number 400 / 300 Must be raised above the app shell. esa-side-dialog is a custom overlay, not a native <dialog>, so it has no top layer and DOM order will not save it. This shell’s topbar is 1100 and its nav flyout 1200, so the record panel runs at 1300 / 1250 and the edit panel above it at 1340 / 1310.
--side-dialog-inset length 16px The gap on all four sides, and the stacking knob: the under card re-points it to 40px while a second panel is open, which the lego animates. The peek is the difference between the two insets, capped at the lego’s 24px body padding — past that the strip slices glyphs off the labels underneath.
--side-dialog-backdrop-bg color rgba(0,0,0,.5) The scrim. The top card of a stack sets it to --bca-panel-stack-scrim (30% black), which dims the card underneath one step without taking the page to the 50% the record panel already applies. It must be this token and not --color-backdrop: the lego falls back to --color-backdrop only when this one is unset, and component-tokens.css sets it at :root — so the substitution has already happened and the concrete colour is what inherits down.
--shadow-400 shadow 0 8px 32px -8px rgba(0,0,0,.08) The panel’s elevation, hardcoded in the lego with no --side-dialog-shadow hook. Both panels re-point it to --bca-panel-shadow: the shipped value throws straight DOWN at 8%, and a right-anchored panel only ever shows its left edge.
--record-* length / weight page scale The density block. bca-record-header, bca-field-group and bca-field all read these, so a panel sets its scale once on the slot scopes rather than threading a prop through three components.
